# Wrapped for the Weekend — project report

**OutsideLLMS 2026 · Outside Lands, Golden Gate Park, Aug 7–9 2026 · mobile web, no install**

Spotify Wrapped for a music festival, built on presence instead of streams.

---

## 1. What it is, in one line

You go to the festival, and the festival hands you back a story.

You take photos and walk around. From nothing but coordinates and timestamps, the system works out which artist you were standing in front of, how long you actually stayed, and what you gave up to be there. On Sunday night it returns a Wrapped — your own photographs, stamped with what was playing, plus a single shareable strip.

---

## 2. The insight

Spotify Wrapped is derived from a stream log. It knows what you *played*. It has no notion of **opportunity cost** — playing a song costs you nothing.

A festival is the opposite. At Outside Lands you stand in front of one stage while four others are running. Attending a set costs you every other set in that window. That tradeoff is a far stronger taste signal than a play count, and nobody captures it.

Our headline statistic is therefore not "your top artist" but **`concurrentSetsSkipped`** — how many other sets were running during a set you chose to attend. Our stats are about sacrifice, which is a genuinely different emotional register.

### Why not a proximity game

Someone at any festival hackathon is building Pokémon Go for the venue. The difference is the reward.

Pokémon Go rewards **arriving**. The reward is generated by the app, identical for everyone, and exists whether or not you cared about what you walked to. It pulls attention *off* the thing you came for.

We reward **being present**. The card does not exist until you take a photo, and your photo *is* the card face. Two people at the same set walk away with different objects. There is nothing to farm, because the artifact is a record rather than a prize. Same proximity mechanic, opposite incentive — which matters to artists and festivals, who dislike anything that turns their crowd into people staring at phones.

### Why the "after"

Most festival software is built for the hours inside the gates — wayfinding, wait-time filler, quests. That lane is crowded and we deliberately do not compete in it. We did not build navigation; the festival's own app already does.

The festival ends Sunday at 10PM and every one of those products goes dark. **Monday morning is unclaimed**, and it is where the real failure lives: you saw eleven artists, you loved four, and by Tuesday you remember two names and follow none of them. The artist played to 75,000 people and converted almost nothing. That gap is the product.

## 3. End to end, how it actually works

```
PHONE (offline-capable)
  camera ──► photo {ts, lat, lng, blob} ──► IndexedDB
  geolocation.watchPosition (throttled 60–90s) ──► sample {ts, lat, lng} ──► IndexedDB
  service worker: app shell + grid cached
        │  (opportunistic sync when signal exists — never blocking)
        ▼
CONVEX
  ingest mutations ──► photos[], dwellSamples[]
        ├─► deterministic resolver
        │     stage = pointInPolygon(lat, lng)
        │     set   = gridLookup(stage, ts)
        │     dwell = groupContiguous(samples)
        │     mint? = eligibility(stage, set, dwell, photo)
        ├─► OpenAI vision (batched, async, beside the fact path)
        └─► reactive queries ──► shelf, mintable-now, wrapped

JAMBASE (fetched once at bootstrap, cached whole)
  festival grid → sets[] with artist, day, headliner flags
  artist metadata → Spotify / MusicBrainz IDs, genres, upcoming tour dates
```

### The five steps of resolution

1. `photo.{lat,lng}` → point-in-polygon against five hand-traced stage polygons → `stageId`
2. `(stageId, photo.ts)` → lookup against the cached JamBase grid → `setId`
3. location samples → contiguous runs → intersected with set windows → `dwellRuns`
4. `eligibility(stage, set, dwell, photo)` → card becomes `AVAILABLE`
5. `setId` → cached JamBase metadata → Spotify ID, next tour date

Every step is arithmetic or a table lookup. There is no model anywhere in this path.

### Capture

Mobile web, no install, opens straight to a camera. No prompts, no tagging, no "which artist is this" — you are at a concert in the dark, and anything requiring reading is a failure.

On capture we write `{clientId, ts, lat, lng, blob}` to IndexedDB **immediately** and never await the network. Location comes from a warm cached position rather than a fresh GPS fix, because a cold fix takes seconds and would stall the shutter. Sync drains opportunistically on a timer, on `online`, and on foreground — never on capture. Every write is idempotent on a client-generated UUID, so a retry can never double-write.

This matters because ~75,000 people are on the grounds daily and the cell network is functionally gone. Most competing entries stop working at 2PM Saturday.

### Stage geometry

Five polygons — Lands End, Twin Peaks, Sutro, SOMA, Duboce Triangle — traced by hand from satellite imagery over the western half of Golden Gate Park. The stages sit in separate meadows (Polo Field, Hellman Hollow, Lindley, Marx), so point-in-polygon is unambiguous; we are not disambiguating two tents 50m apart.

Each polygon carries a buffer in metres. **Duboce Triangle gets 45m — the largest — because it sits in eucalyptus at McLaren Pass and the tree canopy degrades GPS most.** Overlaps resolve to the nearer centroid, deterministically.

**Null is a valid answer.** A sample in transit, at Wine Lands, or at a food vendor resolves to `null` and breaks the dwell streak — which is correct, because walking off to get a drink should end your streak. We never snap a null to the nearest stage; that would manufacture presence.

### Dwell

`watchPosition` throttled to one stored sample per 60–90 seconds — we are resolving meadows, not metres, and a phone at 20% by mid-afternoon cannot afford continuous high-accuracy GPS.

Samples are sorted, resolved, grouped into contiguous runs, denoised (runs under 2 samples dropped), and intersected with set windows. **If the gap between samples exceeds 5 minutes — phone asleep, app backgrounded — we do not interpolate across it.** We close the run and open a new one. Inventing presence we did not observe would violate the core invariant, so we under-report rather than guess.

Thresholds: ≥5 min inside a set window makes a card mintable, ≥10 min counts as "attended," ≥80% of set duration counts as "stayed for the whole set."

### Mint and the card

All five conditions required: inside the polygon, during the set window ±15 min grace, dwell ≥5 min, **at least one photo captured in that window**, and no existing card for that user/set pair. The photo condition is load-bearing — it is what makes the card a record of presence rather than a prize for arriving.

The card is **already earned** by the time the spin appears; the spin only reveals which frame variant you got. Delight without deception, and nothing in the UI ever suggests you might come away empty-handed. `AVAILABLE` persists — leaving without spinning does not forfeit anything.

Five frame variants: `ranger_badge` (park signage), `trail_marker` (wooden trail sign), `fog_layer` (a Karl-the-Fog wash whose intensity scales with set time — August in SF is genuinely fog and cold, so this is literal rather than decorative), `disco_bison` (the joke card, 5% and never boosted, because every set of frames needs one stupid card and that is the one people post), and `field_notes` (reserved for the Wrapped summary). The ranger-and-bison theme is the festival's own 2026 art program identity, not our invention.

**Rarity is looked up, never assigned.** It derives from estimated audience (inverse), festival debut, final show, and whether you skipped a concurrent headliner. A 300-person Duboce Triangle set is genuinely rarer than a Lands End headliner because fewer people were standing there — true, checkable, and far more satisfying than a colour-coded tier someone invented.

Frames are layered templates with slots — photo, treatment, chrome, text, rarity mark — applied at *render* time rather than capture time, so improving a theme retroactively upgrades every card already minted. Cards render 5:7 at 2x on the shelf; the shareable strip renders server-side at 1080×1920.

## 4. Sponsor usage

The division of labour is clean and each sponsor is load-bearing rather than bolted on:

> **JamBase decides what is true. Convex holds what you collected. OpenAI describes what it sees and says it well.**

### 4.1 JamBase — ground truth

**Base URL** `https://api.data.jambase.com/v3`, Bearer auth. Spec at `data.jambase.com/openapi.json` (v3.1.0).

JamBase is the sole authority for who played, where, and when. Every artist name and set window on screen originates here.

**Bootstrap, run once at event start:**

| Call | Purpose | What we got |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /festivals?name=Outside Lands` | resolve the festival | `festivalSeries[0].identifier` = `jambase:13969984`, 19 editions, latest year 2026 |
| `GET /festivals/13969984/events?perPage=100` | pull the edition | 2026 edition `jambase:15738826`, Aug 7–9, Golden Gate Park, **94 performers** |
| `GET /artists/id/jambase:49881` | enrich each artist | `sameAs[]` → Spotify, MusicBrainz, official site; `genre[]` |
| `GET /events?artistId=jambase:49881` | next tour date | 16 upcoming shows with venue and city |

**The grid is cached whole and JamBase is never called again.** Offline resolution depends entirely on the lookup being local — a three-day festival's grid is small enough to hold in memory on a phone. This is an architectural commitment, not an optimisation.

**Third-party ID matching is the sleeper feature.** Artist name → Spotify ID is normally the ugliest part of any music project: fuzzy matching, disambiguation, remaster duplicates, three bands sharing a name. JamBase has already joined it. Charli XCX resolves to `25uiPmTg16RbhZWAqwLBy5` in one field. That single value is what makes the final Wrapped card — *"they play Oakland in November, one tap to hear them first"* — nearly free, and it is the quietest, most convincing moment in the demo. **No Spotify API key or OAuth flow is required anywhere in this product.**

JamBase also supplies the headliner flags used in rarity scoring, genre tags, and event status (a rescheduled or cancelled set must re-resolve rather than silently mismatch).

Because JamBase covers 20,000 festivals and 5M performances, the same architecture works at any show anyone attends — the collection is portable across events, and each festival wears its own theme pack.

**Known limitation, stated plainly:** for this festival the API exposes artists with **day-level** dates, headliner rank, genre and images — but no stage assignment and no set start/end times. Artists, days and headliners in our grid are real and match the live API. Set times and stage assignments are modeled until the by-stage schedule publishes. We carry a `_provenance` field on every record so nothing overclaims by accident.

### 4.2 Convex — state and the shelf

Convex holds what you collected, and does two things that are visible in the pitch rather than buried.

**Reactive subscriptions drive the live shelf.** A judge mints a card and the shelf updates with no refresh; when a second judge mints from the same set on another device, the first judge's screen moves. That is a two-second demo moment you get for free from Convex and cannot get from a REST backend — and it is precisely the moment that demonstrates our differentiator, because two people at one set walk away with visibly different objects.

**Persistence carries the collection across events.** The shelf is not a session; it is a collection that grows festival over festival. You can tell someone has been to six festivals by looking at their shelf.

#### Data model

Eleven tables in `convex/schema.ts`. Every index exists to serve one named access pattern — none are speculative:

| Table | Indexes | Serves |
|---|---|---|
| `users` | `by_deviceId` | identity lookup on every app open |
| `events` | `by_jambaseFestivalId` | join our event row to JamBase's festival |
| `stages` | `by_eventId` | load five polygons for an event |
| `sets` | `by_eventId`, `by_eventId_stageId_startTime` | **the grid lookup** — the hot path for resolving a photo to an artist |
| `photos` | `by_userId_timestamp`, `by_userId_clientId` | resolution scan; idempotent ingest |
| `dwellSamples` | `by_userId_timestamp`, `by_userId_clientId` | contiguous-run grouping; idempotent ingest |
| `dwellRuns` | `by_userId_setId`, `by_userId` | recompute-and-replace on re-run; all runs for `deriveSignals` |
| `cards` | `by_userId_setId`, `by_userId` | **mint dedupe**; shelf newest-first |
| `tasks` | `by_eventId` | active tasks for an event |
| `taskCompletions` | `by_userId_taskId` | completion check without a scan |
| `wrapped` | `by_userId_eventId` | one Wrapped per user per festival |

The `(eventId, stageId, startTime)` composite on `sets` is the one that matters most: it is a range scan over a single stage's day, which is exactly the shape of "what was playing here at this timestamp."

#### Function surface

Sixteen functions across nine modules, and the *kind* of each is a deliberate architectural choice:

**Mutations (client-callable):**
- `users.ensure` — deviceId → user row
- `ingest.photos`, `ingest.samples` — idempotent bulk upsert
- `ingest.generateUploadUrl` — hands the client a one-shot URL so photo blobs stream directly into Convex file storage rather than through a mutation payload
- `mint.claim` — deduped card creation
- `tasks.verify` — completion check
- `grid.bootstrap`, `grid.upsertEvent`, `grid.upsertStages`, `grid.upsertSets` — the JamBase write path

**Queries (reactive — these are live subscriptions, not fetches):**
`queries.grid`, `queries.shelf`, `queries.mintableNow`, `queries.taskProgress`, `queries.wrapped`

**Internal (not reachable from the client):**
- `resolve.run` — an `internalMutation`. Resolution is scheduled server-side after ingest, so the phone never waits on it and no client can trigger a recompute of someone else's facts.
- `wrappedData.persist` — an `internalMutation` that writes the finished Wrapped.

**Action:**
- `wrapped.compute` — a Convex **action** with `"use node"`, because it calls OpenAI and actions are the only function kind permitted to reach external APIs. It reads via `ctx.runQuery(internal.wrappedData.gatherStats)` and writes via `ctx.runMutation(internal.wrappedData.persist)`.

That last split is worth calling out, because it is Convex's transactional model doing real work for us. Mutations are transactional and cannot perform network I/O; actions can perform I/O but are not transactional. By putting the OpenAI call in an action and both the read and the write in transactional internal functions, **a slow or failed model call can never leave a half-written Wrapped behind.** The stats are gathered atomically, the model runs outside the transaction, and the result is persisted atomically. It also enforces the core invariant structurally: the only function that can reach a model is the one that produces prose, and it has no ability to write facts.

#### Idempotency and dedupe

Two correctness properties that the festival's network conditions make non-negotiable:

**Ingest is idempotent on the device-generated `clientId`.** A phone that loses signal mid-drain retries the same batch; the `by_userId_clientId` index makes the existence check a point lookup rather than a scan, so retrying costs nothing and duplicates nothing. Without this index the check would be a table scan on every batch — the difference between a sync that works in a field and one that doesn't.

**Mint is deduped on `(userId, setId)`.** Tapping spin twice, or racing an optimistic client insert against the server, cannot produce two cards for one set. A duplicate card on stage would undermine the "one record per set you actually attended" claim in the most visible way possible.

#### Pure functions, one source of truth

Geometry, dwell arithmetic and mint eligibility live in `lib/geo`, `lib/dwell` and `lib/mint` as pure functions, and the Convex layer *imports* them rather than reimplementing them. This means the same point-in-polygon code runs client-side while offline and server-side during resolution — a correction to a stage polygon has exactly one place to change, and the client and server can never disagree about whether you were at Lands End.

#### Identity

A `deviceId` UUID in localStorage exchanged for a user row via `users.ensure`. No login, no email, no password — nobody is typing credentials at 9PM in a field, and requiring it would cost us most of the captures we exist to collect.

### 4.3 OpenAI — perception and voice

Three jobs, all deliberately **outside the fact path**. Default model `gpt-5.6-sol` via the Responses API.

**Vision — best frame and subject.** Batched per set, never per-photo at capture time, and queued for the resolution pass rather than blocking anything. Input is a burst of photos from one set; output is strict JSON naming the best frame plus a per-photo subject classification (`stage` / `people` / `food` / `scenery`), a quality score, and a blur flag.

The subject field is what makes *"you shot 43 photos of your friends and 6 of the stage"* possible. That trait is unreachable from dwell data alone, and it is one of the lines people quote back. It is also the clearest demonstration of the division of labour: **the model tells us what is in the photo; the coordinate and timestamp tell us where and when it was taken.**

Prompts specify JSON only with no preamble or fences, and the parser strips fences defensively anyway.

**Narrative — the Wrapped voice.** Input is a derived-stats JSON object; output is lines. The system prompt is constrained hard: never compute, infer or estimate a number — use only the numbers given; be descriptive rather than evaluative; no flattery; second person, present tense; one sentence per card.

"You shot 43 photos of your friends" is a fact that becomes a character. A model inventing a personality verdict from nothing — "you're a discovery-driven explorer" — is where these products get cringe, and the prompt forbids it explicitly.

**Task copy.** An artist types one sentence of intent — *"I want people to catch my opener, they always miss it"* — and OpenAI produces the task title, description and reward framing. The artist spends thirty seconds instead of fifteen minutes. Small feature, large usability gain, and it makes the OpenAI usage visible on the artist-facing surface too.

**Graceful degradation is a design requirement, not an accident.** Removing the OpenAI key changes no number anywhere in the product — only prose degrades to deterministic fallbacks. Vision failure falls back to the first photo in the window. This is testable and we test it.

## 5. The core invariant

> **Deterministic code decides what happened. The model only writes the sentence around it.**

Every claim in a Wrapped traces to a coordinate and a timestamp. Dwell time is arithmetic over location samples. Stage resolution is point-in-polygon. Artist resolution is a table lookup against the cached grid. Mint eligibility is a boolean over the above. **If a fact cannot be derived from captured data, it does not appear.**

Where vision fits without breaking this: it classifies what is *in* a photo — sharpness, subject, framing. It never decides *where or when* it was taken. Timestamp and coordinates own that permanently.

**Model describes, never locates.**

Two engineering consequences follow, and both cost us numbers we could have faked:
- Gaps over five minutes are never interpolated, so dwell totals are lower than a smoothed version would report.
- Off-stage points never snap to the nearest stage, so some photos resolve to nothing.

We consider both correct. The product's entire defensible claim is that the numbers are real.

## 6. Artist tasks

Artists assign challenges; fans complete them for rewards. This gives artists a reason to care.

Tasks are deliberately **subordinate to the Wrapped** — an input that shapes what your collection looks like on Monday, not a game played during the festival. If tasks ever read as the headline feature, the pitch has drifted into the lane we chose not to compete in.

**No trivia, no scavenger hunts.** A trivia question is answerable from a couch, so it proves you looked something up, not that you were there. Every task verifies one thing: you physically stood in front of a set. Task types — attend a specific set, catch the opener (`slotIndex = 0`), mint N cards in a day, visit a new stage, stay for ≥80% of a set — are all **queries over cards that already exist**. No self-reporting, no honour system, no new verification code paths.

## 7. Technology

- **Frontend:** Next.js 16 App Router, TypeScript, mobile web, no install. `getUserMedia` for camera, throttled `watchPosition` for location.
- **Offline:** IndexedDB for photos, samples, grid and pending mints; service worker caches the app shell and grid.
- **Backend:** Convex — reactive queries and idempotent mutations.
- **Data:** JamBase v3 REST, cached whole at bootstrap.
- **AI:** OpenAI, batched, outside the fact path.
- **Rendering:** client-side canvas for the shelf, server-side `@napi-rs/canvas` for the 1080×1920 share strip.
- **Quality:** 88 automated tests across geometry, dwell, mint eligibility, rendering and the JamBase client.

## 8. Implementation status

Honest accounting as of the demo.

**Working and verified by running it:**
- Capture to IndexedDB, fully offline, idempotent sync queue
- Stage resolution — point-in-polygon, buffers, overlap tie-break, null handling
- Grid lookup with grace window; artist resolution offline
- Dwell run building, gap handling, derived signals including `concurrentSetsSkipped`
- Mint eligibility, rarity scoring, frame selection
- All five card frames rendering; the 1080×1920 share strip rendering server-side
- The Wrapped experience — opening card, field notes, strip, tour-date close with a live Spotify link
- JamBase client against the live API; OpenAI narrative round-trip end to end
- 88 tests passing, production build clean, 14 routes

**Built but not yet joined:** each path was developed against fixtures. The Convex deployment has not been provisioned with the schema (it requires an interactive login we could not complete during the build), so the shelf currently reads fixture data and the ingest queue drains to a stub rather than the database. The Convex functions, schema and indexes are written and reviewed; wiring them is a short, well-understood task rather than an open design question.

**Known gaps:** set times and stage assignments are modeled (§4.1); the narrative prompt currently produces stat-readout phrasing rather than the intended voice; no vision pass has run against real photographs yet.
