# Wrapped for the Weekend

**OutsideLLMS 2026 — Aug 2, San Francisco. 1PM kickoff, 6PM demos. Mobile web.**

Spotify Wrapped for a music festival, built on presence instead of streams.

---

## The pitch

Spotify Wrapped knows what you played. It doesn't know what you *chose*. At Outside Lands you stand in front of one stage while four others are running, and that tradeoff is a far stronger taste signal than a play count.

We capture it. You take photos, you walk around, you stay somewhere longer than you meant to. From that we mint artist cards, track what actually held you, and hand back a Wrapped on Sunday night.

**One line:** you go to the festival, and the festival hands you back a story.

---

## Positioning

### Against Spotify Wrapped
Wrapped is derived from a stream log. It has no notion of opportunity cost — playing a song costs you nothing. Attending a set costs you every other set in that window. Our stats are about sacrifice, and that is a genuinely different emotional register.

### Against Pokémon Go
Someone at this event is pitching Pokémon Go for the festival. The difference is the reward.

Pokémon Go rewards you for **arriving**. The reward is generated by the app, identical for everyone, and exists whether or not you cared about what you walked to. It pulls attention *off* the thing you came for.

We reward you for **being present**. The card does not exist until you take a photo, and your photo *is* the card face. Two people at the same set walk away with different objects. There is nothing to farm, because the artifact is a record rather than a prize — and the only way to earn it is to actually stand in front of a set.

Same proximity mechanic, opposite incentive. A music-industry judge feels that difference immediately, because artists and festivals hate anything that turns their crowd into people staring at phones chasing spawns.

### Against the on-grounds app field (the during)
Most of the field is building for the hours you are inside the gates — wayfinding, wait-time filler, proximity quests. That is a crowded lane and it is not ours.

**We own the after.** The festival ends Sunday at 10PM and every one of those products goes dark. Monday morning is unclaimed, and it is where the actual failure lives: you saw eleven artists, you loved four, and by Tuesday you remember two names and follow none of them. The artist played to 75,000 people and converted almost nothing.

This is a stated fan need, not a founder's hunch. A regular festival goer describing what is missing at this event asked for exactly this — more engagement *before and after* the event, to connect with others, find new artists, and follow up with rewards for posting on social media. The shareable strip is that reward mechanic, arrived at independently from both directions.

Scope discipline: we do not build the before, and we do not compete for the during. One clean claim.

### Against the offline field
Most entries will be cloud-dependent apps that stop working at 2PM Saturday when the network dies. Ours captures fully offline and resolves later. Owning offline-first is both a real engineering story and the reason the thing survives contact with the park.

## Venue facts that drive design

| Fact | Design consequence |
|---|---|
| Golden Gate Park is 1,017 acres; festival occupies the western half | ~400–500 acre footprint. Stage polygons are meadow-scale. |
| Stages sit in separate meadows: Polo Field, Hellman Hollow, Lindley Meadow, Marx Meadow | Point-in-polygon is unambiguous. We are not disambiguating two tents 50m apart. |
| ~75,000 people on grounds daily (225k over three days) | Cell network is functionally gone. Capture must never block on network. |
| Music noon–10PM, Aug 7–9 2026 | Schedule is bounded and fully known in advance. Grid can be cached whole. |
| Named stages: Lands End, Twin Peaks, Sutro, SOMA, Duboce Triangle | Five polygons plus non-stage zones (Wine Lands, Beer Lands, Grass Lands, Dolores). |
| Duboce Triangle sits in eucalyptus groves at McLaren Pass | Heaviest GPS drift zone. Polygon gets the most generous buffer. |
| Festival's own 2026 art program includes "Bison and Ranger Ruth" | Ranger-and-bison theme is the festival's own identity, not our invention. |
| August in SF: fog, wind, cold | The fog card frame is literal, not decorative. |

## Sponsor mapping

Neither sponsor is bolted on. All three are load-bearing.

| Sponsor | Role | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **OpenAI** (headline) | Perception + voice | Vision: best-frame selection and subject classification. Narrative: derived stats to Wrapped copy. Task copy generation for artists. |
| **JamBase** | Ground truth | Festival grid (who / where / when). Third-party ID matching to Spotify, MusicBrainz, Ticketmaster. Next tour dates. |
| **Convex** | State + shelf | Reactive subscriptions drive the live shelf. Persistence carries the collection across events. |

**The division:** JamBase decides what is true. Convex holds what you collected. OpenAI describes what it sees and says it well.

The event brief reads "Build with OpenAI." OpenAI usage must be visible in the pitch, not buried in a helper function.

## Core invariant

**Deterministic code decides what happened. The model only writes the sentence around it.**

Every claim in a Wrapped traces to a coordinate and a timestamp:
- Dwell time is arithmetic over location samples.
- Stage resolution is point-in-polygon.
- Artist resolution is a table lookup against the cached JamBase grid.
- Mint eligibility is a boolean over the above.

If a fact cannot be derived from captured data, it does not appear.

**Where vision fits without breaking this:** OpenAI vision classifies what is *in* a photo — sharpness, subject, framing. It never decides *where or when* it was taken. Timestamp and coordinates own that permanently. **Model describes, never locates.**

## System architecture

```
PHONE (offline-capable)
  camera ──► photo {ts, lat, lng, blob} ──► IndexedDB
  geolocation.watchPosition (throttled 60–90s) ──► sample {ts, lat, lng} ──► IndexedDB
  service worker: app shell cached, grid cached
        │
        │ (opportunistic sync when signal exists)
        ▼
CONVEX
  ingest mutation ──► photos[], dwellSamples[]
        │
        ├─► resolver (deterministic)
        │     stage = pointInPolygon(lat,lng)
        │     set   = gridLookup(stage, ts)
        │     dwell = groupContiguous(samples)
        │     mint? = eligibility(stage, set, dwell)
        │
        ├─► OpenAI vision (batched, async)
        │     bestFrame(burst) → photoId
        │     subject(photo)   → stage|people|food|scenery
        │
        └─► reactive queries ──► shelf, mintable-now, wrapped

JAMBASE (fetched once at event start, cached)
  festival grid → sets[] with stage, artist, window
  artist metadata → spotifyId, genre, tour dates
```

### Working agreement
- Every path owns a disjoint set of files. The **Files you own** section of each branch README is authoritative. If you need a change in someone else's file, say so in the group chat rather than editing it.
- Contracts in `docs/CONTRACTS.md` are frozen at T+30. Change one only by announcing it — everything downstream is typed against it.
- Each path branch replaces the root `README.md` with its own. That is intentional. When merging a path branch into `main`, keep `main`'s README (`git checkout --ours README.md`) and take everything else.
- Integration happens on `main`. Merge early and often; do not sit on a branch for three hours.

## Stack

- **Frontend:** Next.js (App Router) + TypeScript + Tailwind, deployed on Vercel. Mobile web, no install. `getUserMedia` for camera, `geolocation.watchPosition` throttled for location.
- **Offline:** IndexedDB for photos, samples, grid, pending mints. Service worker caches app shell and grid.
- **Backend:** Convex. Reactive queries drive shelf and mintable-now. Mutations handle ingest and mint.
- **Data:** JamBase v3 REST (`https://data.jambase.com/v3`), cached whole at session start. MCP server available as an alternative access path.
- **Vision + language:** OpenAI, batched, outside the fact path.
- **Card render:** canvas client-side, server-side for the strip.

## Demo plan

The festival is Aug 7–9. The demo is Aug 2. **You are demoing a weekend that has not happened yet.**

### Seeding
- Pull the real 2026 grid from JamBase and load it. The artists and set times on screen are real, which matters — a judge who knows the lineup will check.
- Generate a synthetic user with photos, coordinates, and dwell across all three days. Make the dwell numbers plausible and slightly uneven, since perfectly clean data looks fake.

### Live portion
1. Judge takes a photo in the room.
2. It mints against a mocked set (spoof the coordinate to a stage polygon).
3. Spin animates, card lands on the shelf, shelf updates live via Convex subscription.
4. Hand the phone to a second judge, let them mint a different frame from the same set — this demonstrates that two people at one set get different objects, which is the whole differentiator from Pokémon Go.

### Then the payoff
Switch to the seeded account. "Here is what Sunday night looks like." Swipe the Wrapped, land on the strip.

**Rehearse the seam between live capture and seeded Wrapped.** That transition is where demos die. Know exactly which taps get you there and do not improvise it in front of ten judges.

### What to say about the network
Say out loud that capture is fully offline and that this is deliberate, because the network at a 75,000-person festival is gone. Judges who have worked events — and there is a COFAME judge on the panel — will know that immediately and will respect that you designed for it.

### Lines worth saying verbatim
- "Spotify knows what you played. It doesn't know what you *chose*."
- "We didn't build navigation. The festival app already does that. We built the part nobody owns."
- "Two people at the same set walk away with different objects."
- "Deterministic code decides what happened. The model only writes the sentence around it."

## Getting started

```bash
npm install
npx convex dev          # first run links the Convex project
npm run dev             # http://localhost:3000
```

Camera and geolocation require HTTPS on a real device. Use `npx vercel dev --listen 3000` plus a tunnel, or deploy a preview to Vercel and open it on the phone — do not lose an hour trying to get `getUserMedia` working over plain `http://` on a LAN IP.
